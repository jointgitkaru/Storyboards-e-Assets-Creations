# STORYBOARD SHEET FORMAT (Lock Block 04 — locked)

> The format definition every generated sheet is measured against. Rewritten to 5×3 and fifteen panels, with the chrome clause replacing the old blanket no-text ban.

```
STORYBOARD SHEET FORMAT (locked)

Every storyboard is ONE image: a clean 5×3 grid of fifteen sequential panels on a single card,
read left to right, top to bottom, exactly as the eight reference sheets are laid out.

• Fifteen panels, equal size, evenly spaced, thin clean gutters. Five across, three down. Never 3×3, never nine.
• The panels show ONE continuous sequence progressing in time — panel 1 opens, panel 15 closes.
  Not fifteen variations of the same frame, not fifteen unrelated scenes.
• The subject is IDENTICAL in every panel — same design, colour, materials, proportions.
  Only the moment, pose, angle and framing change.
• Framing varies panel to panel — wide, medium, close-up, extreme macro, low angle, overhead,
  over-the-shoulder, profile, Dutch angle. Never the same framing twice in a row across all fifteen.
• Every panel has real motion — a hand entering, the subject turning, light shifting, fabric moving,
  liquid pouring. Nothing sits still in any of the fifteen.
• Lighting, colour grade, and render style identical across all fifteen panels.
• TIMING: one second per panel. Panel 1 is 00:00–00:01, panel 15 is 00:14–00:15. Total runtime 0:15.
• CHROME: each panel carries its number and its timecode, and one short line of caption text sits
  directly beneath the panel, exactly as the reference sheets do. A header block runs across the top:
  the film's title word on the left, the format and runtime label on the right.
• The panel image itself stays clean. The only text on the sheet is the panel number, the timecode,
  the one line caption and the header block. No watermarks, no logos, no arrows, no speech bubbles,
  no lettering inside the frame.

■■ The sheet must look like fifteen frames pulled from a single continuous shot sequence,
   not fifteen separate images.
```
